How to Deliver Photos to Clients Like a Pro

USB, file links, or a proper client gallery? How wedding photographers deliver images in 2026 — and what clients actually expect from the handover.

Nov 7, 2026

Every wedding workflow has a moment where the photographer's job is technically finished and the client's memory of them is still being written. That moment is delivery. Get it right and a couple who hired you for one day starts sending their engaged friends to you. Get it wrong and you become the talented photographer who was "a bit hard to deal with at the end." Delivery is not logistics. It is the last chapter of the service.

Option one: the USB drive

A genuinely lovely object when it is engraved and boxed — and the option most likely to go wrong in practice: drives fail, envelopes get lost in moves, and the couple's new laptop has no port. Keep the USB as a gift if you love the ritual, never as the only copy.

Option two: the file-sharing link

Fast and cheap, but links expire, folders get buried, and there is no page to send to grandparents. Couples who receive a bare folder often don't realise they're allowed to print, share, or even that delivery is complete.

Option three: the client gallery

A gallery is a page, not a folder. Images load in order, at full quality, on any device. The couple can download everything, share a link with family, and return for years — on anniversaries, to print a canvas, to show their children. This is what clients now expect. A bare folder reads like an unfinished job; a gallery reads like a studio.

What makes a good gallery

Storage that matches your season with headroom to upgrade; downloads the client can actually finish (full-res, bulk, no account gymnastics); a page that stays up — an expiring link creates the worst email a photographer can send; and privacy in writing — we do not sell your data, and we do not use your photographs to train AI models.

A simple delivery workflow

  1. Cull and edit with the gallery in mind — sequence the day the way it happened.
  2. Upload within the contract's timeframe, ideally beating it by a week.
  3. Send one email with one link, a download note, a printing note.
  4. Follow up once, thirty days later. That final follow-up is where referrals quietly happen.
